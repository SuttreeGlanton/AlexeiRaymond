title: Man’s Anguish
fullText: true
---

<p>This morning I was kicked out of my lodging—a sterile studio apartment bearing the name <em>La Svolta</em>—due to the landlord’s insistence on letting in a cleaning lady. Not trusting me. So, after a brief morning rush, when I failed to be ready in time—the cleaner knocking, muffle-muttering, me imploring, <em>un attimo, attimo, scusa, cinque minuti</em>—I found myself strolling down Via Vanchiglia in an early summery just-before-noon, weighed down by muggy heat, catching distasteful glimpses of myself while passing by vitrines, weaving between elderlies on their late-morn errands. But now, look, the way this young woman’s pushing her stroller, her long blue lemon-speckled skirt. I’m sure her flesh would tremble delightfully from the strength of her step if it weren’t for the taut, constricting garment. She must have it all figured out. No husband in sight, but it doesn’t seem like anything, or anyone, is missing. And here to my left, sitting squat below a tree in perfect shade is another woman, solo, likely a student, book at hand, gaze downturned. But where is the one I followed accidentally? She was simply bound in the same direction—I meant nothing by it, and is it so bad that I happened to have enjoyed the sight? Our paths diverged soon enough when I turned in the direction of the museum, and out she went from my thoughts. But this store now, no, a groomer’s. <em>Poodles Only? </em>Are there really enough poodles in Torino to allow such an exclusionary business? Let’s enumerate canine varieties; Spaniels, Terriers, Collies… My apologies for these amateur flâneur’s vagaries. They’re to mask a form of embarrassment I feel as I carry myself through these broad, long streets, listening in, peeping. And now, almost in a flash, I’m under the <em>portici</em>, as toward me rushes a wave of people, some mixture of fellow non-Italians and the regal Turinese themselves, where among them a single face stands out—she’s chewing on something, likely delicious, but her countenance remains queenly, and she sends a nonchalant side-eye toward me. She is pretty, and likely judgmental. As opposed to me, of course. With the last of her blonde impressions passing from my mind, I look onward, to the emerging skyline above the Savoy dynasty’s central piazza, where the Antonelliana is a dark-grey stiletto coming up among lesser buildings. A cut above. I pass through a narrower street, where pedestrians barely avoid the brotherly shoulder-to-shoulder, a tap, a brush, and I choose to relinquish the sidewalk in favor of a trio—older gentlemen conjoined in a kind of reunion I cannot help but envy. Already their voices fade behind, and still I wish I'd known them, could greet with a <em>ciao, tutto bene? Long time no see, let’s have a seat, tell me all about how life’s been an utter treat!</em> But don’t worry, I won’t bore you with the minutiae of every step and sight. In a matter of minutes, more musings—if I manage to muster up the wherewithal to pay attention and process on the fly—I will end up seated at a café. Yes, I do take a seat, despite hesitation, the urge to duck away, quicken my step, swallow my thirst in senseless, habitual self-deprival. I order a violet spritz to counteract the sizzling sun’s searing of me, then double down with a Hugo spritz, and for half an hour all aligns, my worries are chased away by iced purple, greenish liquids, from the mind. Even higher desires bubble up, the confidence to chase them down, as projections flash onto the street, the passersby. I imagine myself here, there, with genuine delight, the spine and its tingle. Is this all it takes to fall into the rhythm of life? A mere two spritzes, some green olives to skewer and leisurely bring toward my sere lips? I’ve had my fill, so I pay and surmise enough time had idly gone by. It’s time to walk back and hope the dear lady has already departed. But before I let myself finally lock into unthinking motion, I notice a bicycled man approaching, only he’s not alone. His toddler daughter rides with him, on a seat behind, wielding a pink plastic pistol. As her father rides, she takes aim, shooting all those they cycle by, glistening bubbles coming out, losing track of the crowd, trailing up, up, till a breeze makes them pop, collide, and soapily sigh. I am of those the little girl aims to bubbly wound, and as the bubbles mostly spin past, a few do graze my skin. I find them pleasant, these cold bursts, and after examining my wrist, I look up and see her growing distant, though not before making out her wink, her mischievous, knowing smile. <em>I shot you down, </em>she transmits, and the sight arrests as it alludes to that which eludes. But here, I’m back on Via Vanchiglia, none the wiser, the walk having done little to advance my soul’s affairs. </p>

<p>So, Dear Reader, I admit I failed to convey a proper ending and culmination to this measly journey. I tried, believe me, but perhaps, if you could hear the music ringing in my ears, it would have satisfied in lieu, making you feel this abandonment I live as I’m hurled from day to night to day again. Ah, but with keys at hand what’s left is to simply turn a final corner, which, nigh as an automaton, I do, but I almost walk into a man clutching his face with one hand, his startled, horrified eyes visible just above the fingers, betraying anguish, seeing me. Pure anguish. Bushy moustache, creased forehead, and red, red eyes. I hurry past. </p>

<p>It’s him, the culmination. God forgive me—</p>
