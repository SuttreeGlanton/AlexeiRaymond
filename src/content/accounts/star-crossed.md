---
title: Star-Crossed
fullText: true
---

<p>We’re arguing, again. The plan was to walk together, and <span data-sc="0">she was late</span>. Or maybe I was <span data-sc="1">late</span> for good reason, and she made a big deal of it. <span data-sc="2">She never understands me</span>. No, yeah, <span data-sc="3"><em>she’s</em> the one who was late</span>. <span data-sc="4">I had to mill about in the street while she kept texting me</span> <em>hold on</em>, <em>wait another minute</em>, and another. It’s like she’s unwilling to see things from my perspective; <span data-sc="5">she just knows how to fight</span>. And anyway, the night’s already <span data-sc="6">sour</span>.</p>

<p>We’re walking <span data-sc="7">briskly</span>. She has her <span data-sc="8">white</span> sneakers on—no, her <span data-sc="9">pink</span> ones—and I’m wearing my old <span data-sc="10">black</span> pair. <span data-sc="11">She stays ahead of me, probably out of spite</span>. <span data-sc="12">Wait, or is she lagging behind?</span> <span data-sc="13">I don’t have the patience for this; I catch up</span>. <span data-sc="14">I try to keep my voice down when I beg her</span>, <span data-sc="15"><em>just please cooperate</em></span>. We can squash this, and there’s no need for the <span data-sc="16">petulant</span> display. <span data-sc="17">Why does she always do this?</span> <span data-sc="18">I walk ahead and suddenly realize she’s drifting behind</span>, again. <span data-sc="19">Great</span>.</p>

<p><span data-sc="20">She’s still bristling because I took longer to get ready</span>, or because <span data-sc="21">I bugged her about her delay</span>. <span data-sc="22">I remembered our plan</span>; <span data-sc="23">it’s just that things pop up and I can’t always control them</span>. <span data-sc="24">And now she huffs ahead of me as if it’s me who messed up our night</span>. <span data-sc="25">Yeah, because I just do that</span>.</p>

<p>She’s wearing a <span data-sc="26">white</span> graphic tee and <span data-sc="27">black</span> leggings—the contrast between her hair and the <span data-sc="28">white</span> shirt is always nice. I can admit that despite <span data-sc="29">her tantrum</span>. I mean, she does look <span data-sc="30">good</span> in that <span data-sc="31">red</span> shirt—it was always <span data-sc="32">her color</span>. I look down at my chest and see how my shirt is already dark with sweat. <span data-sc="33"><em>Perfect</em></span><em> summer night out</em>. <span data-sc="34">I doubt my mood will recover</span>. We’re at odds and she’s the most <span data-sc="35">stubborn</span> girl I’ve ever known. <span data-sc="36">She grows louder and I go deeper into silence</span>. Actually, it’s the reverse. <span data-sc="37">I can never pull her out of her insistent silence when she’s like this—she just won’t budge</span>. And yeah, <span data-sc="38">I try to keep my voice down because I don’t want to cause a scene and have people gawk at us, but she’s so frustrating that I just can’t help myself.</span></p>

<p>Oh, there she goes—she’s almost <span data-sc="39">shouting</span> now, and I catch up and <span data-sc="40">harshly whisper</span>, begging her to <span data-sc="41">keep her voice down</span>. <em>Please, please!</em> Why do we always end up having these outbursts while we’re outside? I ignore whoever might be—<em>must</em> be—looking at us by now. We’re that <span data-sc="42">crazy</span> couple disrupting the night. <span data-sc="43">But she doesn’t care, she’s just openly furious now and there’s no way to salvage anything</span>. We’re still walking, and the streets blur past us. We’re heading <span data-sc="44">north</span>, toward the <span data-sc="45">train station</span>, or rather, we’re going <span data-sc="46">west</span> to the <span data-sc="47">beach</span>. <span data-sc="48">It wasn’t meant to be this way</span>. <span data-sc="49">I catch myself, take a deep breath</span>. <span data-sc="50">She insists I’m shouting at her, that it’s scaring her</span>. <span data-sc="51">I resent her for making it seem like I’m shouting</span>. <span data-sc="52">But—I <em>do</em> want to be more in control of myself, so I breathe and speak softer.</span> <span data-sc="53">She’s a bit more responsive now that I’m quieter</span>, but the <span data-sc="54">yelp of a distant cat</span> derails my train of thought. In any case, it looks like <span data-sc="55">she’s run out of things to yell at me</span>.</p>

<p>We enter an empty basketball court—I don’t know why—and circle each other. <span data-sc="56">I avoid her eyes and look at my</span> <span data-sc="57">new, grey sneakers</span>. <span data-sc="58">I can’t bear her acidic glare</span>. <span data-sc="59">I try to catch her eyes but she’s doing everything she can to avoid me</span>. How will we solve this if we won’t even look at each other? She’s still so <span data-sc="60">pretty</span>.</p>

<p><span data-sc="61">I—or is it her?—end the lull and approach</span>. <span data-sc="62">I feel myself slipping into a kind of silence she won’t be able to pull me out of.</span> <span data-sc="63">No matter what I say, I just can’t break through to her,</span> <span data-sc="64">she’s still insisting on my fault, her innocence.</span> We’re at an impasse and <span data-sc="65">I want to go home.</span></p>

<p data-sc-stable>“Valentin, Valentin! Look!”</p>

<p>He stands apart from her; his white shirt soaked through. His eyes are boring helplessly into his scuffed, black shoes. He can’t look up. A pity.</p>

<p>“Oh my God, look! Look!” Her voice bright, free of conflict.</p>

<p>He looks, and she stands there joyous, delightful—the black shirt he gave her hugs her beautifully—as her eyes are fixed on the dark, pin-pricked heavens and now so are his, but—</p>

<p>“What? What is it?”</p>

<p>He scans the night sky, stelliferous but still. He is doubtful, but she genuinely glowing. His furrowed brow relaxes despite his will.</p>

<p>“It was a shooting star!”</p>

<p>“Really? You saw it?”</p>

<p>She closes the distance to him as if there was never any tension. She hugs him.</p>

<p>“Mhm, it just crossed the sky. I’m sorry you missed it.”</p>

<p>He tenses up, worries about sweat. Her cool skin is lovely.</p>

<p>“I—it’s fine. I was just staring at the ground like an idiot.” Tension leaves his body.</p>

<p>She holds on, unbothered by sweat, and his frown is undone next. Something far above gently pulls at the corners of his mouth.</p>

<p>“It’s okay. I can describe it to you.”</p>

<p>Softly, as they turn back from the direction of the station, Noa tells Valentin of the trail left by their final star.</p>
